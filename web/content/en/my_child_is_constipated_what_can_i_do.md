## What it is
Constipation means having fewer than three bowel movements a week, and the stool can be hard and dry [3]. It is also likely if your child has not had a poo at least 3 times in the last week, or is pooing less often than usual [2]. The poo may be unusually large or small, dry, hard, or lumpy [2]. Your child might strain or feel pain when having a poo, or feel like they haven't fully emptied their bowels [2]. They may also have a stomach ache and feel bloated or sick [2]. Almost everyone gets constipated at some point, and in most cases it lasts a short time and is not serious [3].

Common causes include not eating enough fibre, not drinking enough fluids, not moving enough, ignoring the urge to go to the toilet, or a change in diet or daily routine [5]. It can also be a side effect of medicine or related to stress, anxiety, or depression [5].

## What you can do at home
It is safe to try simple diet and lifestyle changes, and you may notice a difference within a few days, though sometimes it takes a few weeks [4].

- **Add fibre gradually:** Include fruits that contain sorbitol, such as apples, apricots, grapes (and raisins), raspberries, and strawberries [8]. Increase the fibre in the diet slowly and add wheat bran, oats, or linseed [8].
- **Increase fluids:** Make sure your child drinks plenty of water and other fluids [8]. Avoid alcohol (for older children/teens) [8].
- **Improve the toilet routine:** Keep to a regular time and place for toileting and give plenty of time [9]. Do not delay if your child feels the urge to poo [9]. Resting feet on a low stool can help; if possible, raise the knees above the hips [9].
- **Increase activity:** A daily walk or run can help your child poo more regularly [9].
- **Consider other habits:** Ensure your child gets enough exercise and takes time to have a bowel movement when needed [3]. Eating more fruits, vegetables, and grains that are high in fibre can help prevent constipation [3].

If diet and lifestyle changes are not helping, speak to a pharmacist [7]. They can suggest a suitable laxative, which is a medicine that helps your child poo more regularly [7]. Most laxatives work within 3 days and should only be used for a short time [7]. Do not use laxatives unless a health care provider says you should [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
See a GP (doctor) if your child:
- Is constipated and it's not getting better with treatment [7].
- Is regularly constipated [7].
- Is regularly bloated [7].
- Has blood in their poo [7].
- Has lost weight without trying [7].
- Is constipated and feels tired all the time [7].
- Is taking medicine that's causing constipation, such as opioid painkillers [7].
- Has sudden changes in bowel habits [7].
- Has tummy pain [7].

Long-term constipation can lead to faecal impaction, where poo builds up in the rectum [6]. The main symptom is diarrhoea that leaks through the build-up of solid poo [6]. This may need treatment from a healthcare professional [6].

## Common questions
**Is it serious if my child doesn't poo every day?**
It's not important to have a bowel movement every day [3]. However, if bowel habits change, check with your provider [3].

**How long do laxatives take to work?**
Most laxatives work within 3 days [7]. They should only be used for a short time [7].

**Can constipation cause other problems?**
Long-term constipation can lead to faecal impaction, where poo builds up in the rectum and diarrhoea leaks around it [6]. This requires medical treatment [6].
