## What it is
Conjunctivitis is the medical name for pink eye. It is inflammation of the outer layer of the eye and the inside of the eyelid [1]. It can cause swelling, itching, burning, discharge, and redness [1]. Causes include bacterial or viral infection, allergies, irritating substances, and contact lens products [1]. Pink eye usually does not affect vision [1]. Infectious pink eye spreads easily from person to person [1]. Most cases clear without medical care, but bacterial pink eye needs antibiotic eye drops or ointment [1]. Viral and allergic conjunctivitis do not respond to antibiotics [5]. Some sexually transmitted infections can cause conjunctivitis, and this type takes longer to get better [5].

## What you can do at home
Conjunctivitis usually gets better in a couple of weeks without treatment [2]. You can ease symptoms at home:
- Wash your eyelids with clean water. Boil the water and let it cool, then gently wipe your eyelashes with a clean cotton wool pad to clean off crusts. Use one piece for each eye [2].
- Hold a cold flannel on your eyes for a few minutes to cool them down [2].
- Do not wear contact lenses until your eyes are better [2].
- A pharmacist can suggest eyedrops or antihistamines to help with symptoms [2]. For a child under 2 years old, you need a prescription from a GP [2].

To stop infectious conjunctivitis from spreading:
- Wash your hands regularly with warm soapy water [4].
- Wash pillowcases and face cloths in hot water and detergent [4].
- Cover your mouth and nose when sneezing, and put used tissues in the bin [4].
- Do not share towels and pillows [4].
- Do not rub your eyes [4].

You do not need to stay away from school unless your child is feeling very unwell [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
See a GP if:
- Your baby has red, sticky eyes. Get an urgent appointment if your baby is less than 30 days old [3].
- You wear contact lenses and have conjunctivitis symptoms plus spots on your eyelids — you might be allergic to the lenses [3].
- Symptoms have not cleared up within 7 days [3].

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get medical advice if you have:
- Pain in your eyes [3]
- Sensitivity to light [3]
- Changes in your vision, like wavy lines or flashing [3]
- Very red eyes (1 eye or both eyes) [3]
- A baby less than 30 days old with red, sticky eyes [3]

These can be signs of a more serious eye problem [3]. Get medical advice if you need help for a child under 5 [3].

## Common questions
**Can my child go to school with pink eye?**
You do not need to stay away from school unless your child is feeling very unwell [3]. But wash hands often and do not share towels or pillows [4].

**Will antibiotics help?**
Antibiotics only work for bacterial conjunctivitis. They will not work if the cause is a virus or an allergy [5].

**How long does it last?**
Most cases get better in a couple of weeks without treatment [2]. If symptoms have not cleared within 7 days, see a GP [3].
