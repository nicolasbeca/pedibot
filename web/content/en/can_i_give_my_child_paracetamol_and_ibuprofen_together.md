## What the guidelines say

| Organisation | What it says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NHS | Paracetamol can help with aches, pains, teething, earache, sore throat, cold and flu symptoms, and distress from a high temperature. | [2] |
| NHS | Do not give ibuprofen and paracetamol at the same time. You may alternate them only if a doctor or nurse tells you to. If advised to alternate, write down when you gave each medicine to space doses evenly and avoid giving too much. Ibuprofen can be given 3 to 4 times in 24 hours, depending on age and dose — check the packet or leaflet. Your child can eat and drink normally with paracetamol. | [1] |

## Where they agree

The NHS is the only organisation in the sources, so there is no disagreement between bodies. Within its own advice, the key points are consistent: paracetamol is useful for fever and pain, and it should not be combined with ibuprofen at the same moment. The guidance stresses that alternating is not a routine option — it requires a health professional’s instruction. The NHS also emphasises careful record-keeping if you do alternate, to prevent accidental overdose.

## Where they differ (and why it may not matter)

Because only one organisation is present, there are no differences to compare. However, note that the NHS makes a clear distinction between “at the same time” (never) and “alternating” (only with professional advice). This may seem like a fine line, but it matters: giving both together increases the risk of side effects, while alternating under guidance is meant to keep symptoms controlled without exceeding safe doses. If you are unsure whether your child’s symptoms need both medicines, the safest step is to ask a doctor or nurse rather than decide on your own.

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

The sources do not address when to seek medical care for fever. They only cover how to give paracetamol and ibuprofen. If your child has a fever that is not improving, seems very unwell, is under three months old with a temperature, or you are worried for any reason, contact your doctor or go to the emergency department — but this advice comes from general knowledge, not from the provided sources. Always follow the dosing instructions on the medicine packet or leaflet, and never give more than the recommended amount.
