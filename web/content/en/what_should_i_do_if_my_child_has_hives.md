## What it is

Hives are an itchy rash made of raised bumps or patches in many shapes and sizes [2]. The rash can appear anywhere on the body, stay in one area, or spread [2]. It may feel itchy, sting, or burn [2]. On white skin, hives often look pink or red; on brown or black skin, the colour can be harder to see [2]. The bumps can look puffy, like blisters, and may be close together or far apart [1].

Hives happen when something triggers high levels of histamine and other chemicals in the skin [4]. Common triggers include eating certain foods, contact with plants, animals, chemicals, or latex, cold water or wind, hot or sweaty skin, reactions to medicines, insect bites or stings, scratching or pressure on the skin, infections, or rarely water or sunlight [4]. In children, most hives are caused by an infection; an allergic cause is less common [5]. The raised patches are often evanescent, meaning they appear in one area while fading from another [5]. Hives may also come with swelling of the hands, feet, elbows, or face [5].

Hives rashes usually get better within a few days [2]. Outbreaks often last 5 to 7 days, though they can continue for several weeks without being more serious [6].

## What you can do at home

- Watch for any trouble swallowing or breathing [7].
- Avoid rubbing the skin and avoid baths or showers with very hot water [7].
- Do not put powders or creams on the skin without asking your child’s doctor [7].
- Do not give any syrups or medicines without asking your child’s doctor [7].
- Your child can eat their usual food, but do not introduce new foods during the hives [7].
- Try to figure out what triggered the hives so you can avoid that trigger if possible [4].

A pharmacist can give advice about antihistamine treatment for hives [3]. Tell the pharmacist about any long-term conditions, because antihistamines may not be suitable [3]. This treatment might not be suitable for young children [3]. A GP might prescribe menthol cream, antihistamines, or steroid tablets if needed [3]. If hives do not go away with treatment, your child may be referred to a skin specialist [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if [3]:
- Symptoms of hives do not improve after 2 days [3]
- You are worried about your child’s hives [3]
- The rash is spreading [3]
- Hives keep coming back (your child may be allergic to something) [3]
- Your child also has a high temperature and feels unwell [3]
- Your child also has swelling under the skin (this might be angioedema) [3]

Call 999 if [3]:
- Your child’s lips, mouth, throat, or tongue suddenly become swollen [3]
- Your child is breathing very fast or struggling to breathe, may become very wheezy, or feels like they are choking or gasping for air [3]
- Your child’s throat feels tight or they are struggling to swallow [3]
- Your child’s skin, tongue, or lips turn blue, grey, or pale (on brown or black skin, this may be easier to see on the palms of the hands or soles of the feet) [3]
- Your child suddenly becomes very confused, drowsy, or dizzy [3]
- Your child faints and cannot be woken up [3]
- Your child is limp, floppy, or not responding like normal (their head may fall to the side, backwards, or forwards, or they may find it hard to lift their head or focus on your face) [3]

These can be signs of a serious allergic reaction and may need immediate treatment in hospital [3]. Also go to the emergency department if your child has trouble breathing, has a hoarse voice or wheezing sounds in the chest, cannot swallow or drools a lot, feels unwell, or their face swells [6]. Seek urgent care if hives appear after an insect sting or after eating a food that has caused an allergic reaction before [6].

## Common questions

**How long will the hives last?**  
Hives rashes usually get better within a few days [2]. Outbreaks often last 5 to 7 days, but they can continue for several weeks without meaning the condition is more severe [6]. In most cases, no tests are needed, but mention it to your child’s regular doctor at the next check-up [6].

**Can I give my child antihistamines?**  
A pharmacist can advise about antihistamine treatment [3]. This treatment might not be suitable for young children [3]. Always ask a pharmacist or doctor before giving any medicine, and do not give syrups without consulting your child’s doctor [7].

**Is hives always an allergy?**  
No. In childhood, most hives are caused by an infection [5]. An allergic cause is less common [5]. Triggers can also include cold, heat, pressure, or medicines [4].
