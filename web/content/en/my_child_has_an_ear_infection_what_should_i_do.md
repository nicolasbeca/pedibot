## What it is

An ear infection, also called otitis media, is an infection and inflammation of the middle ear, which is located behind the eardrum [4]. It often happens as a complication of a cold with a lot of nasal mucus [4]. It is one of the most frequent infections in children under 2 years old [4]. Children are more likely than adults to get ear infections [8]. Three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday [9].

The most common symptom is ear pain, which can start suddenly and is often worse at night [2]. Babies may be irritable and rub or pull at their ear [2]. They may also have a fever, hear worse, vomit, or have a poor appetite [2]. Increased pressure in the middle ear can cause a small hole in the eardrum, which may lead to pus or blood in the ear canal; usually, the pain improves after this happens [2]. Other signs can include a feeling of pressure or fullness in the ear, itching, and scaly skin in and around the ear [7]. Young children may not react to some sounds, be restless, or keep losing their balance [7].

Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although symptoms can sometimes last up to a week [7]. Often, they go away on their own [9].

## What you can do at home

Treating the pain is essential [1][5]. You can use ibuprofen or paracetamol regularly every 6-8 hours for the first 1-2 days, before the pain appears [1]. For dosing, use the calculator or ask your pharmacist or doctor.

- Do not put anything in the ear to relieve pain [1].
- Avoid getting the ear wet, especially if it has been draining pus [1].
- If your child is a baby, do frequent nasal washes with saline solution [1].
- It is normal for your child to have a poor appetite; do not force them to eat [1].
- If an antibiotic has been prescribed, follow the instructions correctly (dose, interval, and duration) [1].
- If your child has a high temperature or does not feel well enough to do normal activities, try to stay at home and avoid contact with others until they feel better [7].

To help prevent ear infections, frequent hand washing is the most effective measure [5]. Breastfeeding and routine vaccinations, including the flu vaccine, also protect against ear infections [5]. Passive smoking and using a pacifier after 6 months of age can make them more likely [5].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Symptoms usually resolve within 48-72 hours with pain treatment, without needing antibiotics [3]. It is advisable to consult your pediatrician if your child is under 6 months old, has a poor general condition, has a fever of 39ºC or higher, has pus draining from the ear, has had previous episodes of otitis, or has not improved with the prescribed pain treatment after 48-72 hours [3].

Go to an emergency department if your child has any of the following:

- Poor general condition, intense headache, is listless, sleepy, or very irritable [3].
- Redness, pain, or swelling behind the ear, or the ear is displaced forward [3].

Also watch for these signs that may indicate your child is getting worse: poor general condition, intense headache, or redness, pain, or swelling behind the ear [3].

If your child is well and has no fever, they can attend school and do physical activity [3]. If they have a fever, it is recommended that 24 hours pass without fever before returning to school [3].

## Common questions

**Can my child go to school?**
If your child is well and has no fever, they can attend school and do physical activity [3]. If they have a fever, it is recommended that 24 hours pass without fever before returning to school [3].

**Will my child need antibiotics?**
Often, ear infections go away on their own [9]. Your doctor may recommend pain relievers [9]. Severe infections and infections in young babies may require antibiotics [9]. Your pediatrician will determine if an antibiotic is needed [3].

**What if my child has frequent ear infections?**
Children who get infections often may need surgery to place small tubes inside their ears [9]. The tubes relieve pressure in the ears so the child can hear again [9].
