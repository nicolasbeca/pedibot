## What it is

Dengue is a viral infection caused by the dengue virus, which is spread to people through the bite of infected mosquitoes [3]. It is more common in tropical and subtropical climates than in temperate ones, mostly in urban and semi-urban areas [3][4]. About half of the world's population is now at risk, with an estimated 100–400 million infections each year [3].

Most people who get dengue have mild or no symptoms and get better in 1–2 weeks [1][4]. If symptoms do appear, they usually start 4–10 days after infection and last 2–7 days [1]. They may include high fever (40°C/104°F), severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pains, nausea, vomiting, swollen glands and rash [1]. Someone who is infected for a second time is at greater risk of severe dengue [1].

There is no specific treatment for dengue [3][6]. Pain can be managed with paracetamol (acetaminophen) [6]. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory medicines such as ibuprofen and aspirin should be avoided, because they can increase the risk of bleeding [6]. ## What you can do at home

- Rest and give plenty of liquids [7].
- Use paracetamol (acetaminophen) for pain [7].
- Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory medicines such as ibuprofen and aspirin [7].
- Watch for severe symptoms and contact your doctor as soon as possible if you notice any [7].
- Protect your child from mosquito bites, because the mosquitoes that spread dengue are active during the day [7]. Use clothes that cover as much of the body as possible, mosquito repellents (containing DEET, Picaridin or IR3535), window screens, and mosquito nets — ideally sprayed with insect repellent — if sleeping during the day [7].
- To stop mosquitoes breeding: cover, empty and clean domestic water storage containers every week, dispose of solid waste properly, and remove artificial habitats that can hold water [7].

After recovery, people who have had dengue may feel tired for several weeks [1].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

The symptoms of severe dengue often come after the fever has gone away [1]. People with these symptoms should seek care immediately [1]:

- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting
- Rapid breathing
- Bleeding gums or nose
- Fatigue
- Restlessness
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Being very thirsty
- Pale and cold skin
- Feeling weak

Early detection and access to proper medical care greatly lower the death rate of severe dengue [3]. In severe cases, hospital care is often needed [6].

## Common questions

**How does dengue spread?**
Through the bite of infected female mosquitoes, mainly Aedes aegypti [9]. A mosquito can also become infected by biting a person who has the virus — including someone who has no symptoms yet, or none at all [2]. This can happen up to 2 days before symptoms appear and up to 2 days after the fever goes away [2].

**Can my child catch dengue from someone else?**
The main way dengue passes between humans is through mosquito bites [8]. Rare cases have been recorded through blood products, organ donation and transfusions [8]. There is also evidence of possible transmission from a pregnant mother to her baby, though this appears to be rare [8].

**Is there a vaccine?**
There is a vaccine that helps protect against dengue [10]. According to the NHS, it is recommended for people who have had, or might have had, dengue in the past and are travelling to a part of the world where dengue is common [10]. One vaccine (QDenga) is licensed in some countries and is recommended only for those aged 6–16 years in high transmission settings [7]. Ages and recommendations change from country to country, so ask a travel clinic or your doctor for advice if you are travelling to an area where dengue is common [10]. If you need the vaccine, you may have to pay for it privately [10]. The dose is the one your doctor, your pharmacist or the leaflet gives.
