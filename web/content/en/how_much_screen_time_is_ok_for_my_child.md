## What it is

Screen time means time spent passively watching screen-based entertainment like TV, videos, or computer games. It does not include active screen-based games where movement is required [8]. The World Health Organization has set clear limits for children under 5 years of age [1].

For infants under 1 year, screen time is not recommended at all [5][4]. For 1-year-olds, sedentary screen time is also not recommended [2][4]. For children aged 2 years, screen time should be no more than 1 hour per day, and less is better [2][4]. For children 3–4 years of age, screen time should also be no more than 1 hour per day, and less is better [1][4].

These limits are part of a bigger picture. The guidelines look at a full 24-hour day, combining physical activity, sedentary time, and sleep [3]. The greatest health benefits come from meeting all three recommendations together [3]. Less screen time and more physical activity are linked to better motor development and fitness in children 1–4 years old [3]. Longer sleep plus less screen time is linked to better cognitive development and healthy weight [3].

## What you can do at home

- Replace screen time with reading and storytelling with your child [2][4].
- Encourage active play. For young children, physical activity includes walking, crawling, running, jumping, balancing, climbing, dancing, and riding wheeled toys [8].
- Do not keep your child restrained for more than 1 hour at a time in prams, strollers, high chairs, or strapped to your back [2][4].
- Let your child have quiet play that is not screen-based, like sitting and reading a book or playing a calm game [8].
- Remember that quiet sitting time during meals is normal and expected [3].

For sleep, children 1–2 years old need 11–14 hours of good quality sleep including naps, with regular sleep and wake times [1]. Children 3–4 years old need 10–13 hours of good quality sleep, which may include a nap, with regular sleep and wake times [1]. Infants under 1 year need 14–17 hours (0–3 months) or 12–16 hours (4–11 months) of good quality sleep including naps [5].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

These guidelines are for healthy children. If your child has a disability or a medical condition, you may need extra guidance from a health professional [5][7]. The recommendations may still be appropriate, but talk to your doctor or early intervention services for advice specific to your child [7].

## Common questions

**Is any screen time okay for a 1-year-old?**
No. For 1-year-olds, sedentary screen time such as watching TV or videos or playing computer games is not recommended [2][4].

**Does reading count as screen time?**
No. When your child is sedentary, reading and storytelling with a caregiver is encouraged [2][4]. This is different from screen-based entertainment.

**What counts as physical activity for a toddler?**
Physical activity includes walking, crawling, running, jumping, balancing, climbing, dancing, and riding wheeled toys [8]. For children 1–2 years old, the goal is at least 180 minutes of physical activity spread throughout the day [1].

**What if my child uses screens for video calls with family?**
The sources do not specifically address video calls. They define screen time as passively watching screen-based entertainment [8].

**Do these rules apply to children with disabilities?**
The guidelines do not specifically address children with disabilities or chronic disease. Parents should seek additional advice from health professionals or early intervention services [7][9].
