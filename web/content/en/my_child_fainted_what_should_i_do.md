## What it is
Fainting, also called syncope, is a sudden, brief loss of consciousness [2]. It happens when not enough blood or oxygen reaches the brain [2]. Before fainting, your child may feel sick to their stomach, have blurred vision, belly pain, sweat, or hear ringing in their ears [1]. They may look pale, stop responding, and lose strength, possibly falling to the ground [1]. Sometimes, they may have 2 or 3 jerks of the arms or legs as they lose consciousness [1].

Certain situations can trigger fainting [2]. These include extreme heat, pain, fear, nervousness or anxiety, standing still for a long time, or getting up too quickly from lying down [2]. Less common causes include low blood sugar in children with diabetes, some poisonings, heart disease, and dehydration [2]. In general, fainting is not caused by serious medical problems and does not need special testing [3]. It is not a disease [3].

## What you can do at home
If your child starts to feel faint, have them sit or lie down right away to prevent a fall [4]. Changing position is often enough to stop the fainting [4]. Encourage them to breathe slowly and deeply [4].

If your child loses consciousness, lay them down with their legs raised and their head turned to the side so they can breathe better [4]. After the discomfort and weakness pass, they can get up slowly [4]. If they are lying down, they should first sit up and then, after a few minutes, stand [4]. Children usually recover quickly, within a couple of minutes, after a period of rest [4].

To help prevent fainting in the future, you can do the following [3]:
- Avoid or change situations that you know cause fainting. For example, do not get up suddenly from sitting or lying down [3].
- If your child often feels dizzy when having blood drawn, tell the nurse beforehand so your child can lie down during the procedure [3].
- Encourage your child to drink more fluids, especially during physical activity [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Go to an emergency department if any of the following happens [5]:
- Your child takes more than 3-5 minutes to regain consciousness. If this happens, place them in the recovery position and call emergency services [5].
- The fainting happened during physical exercise, during an effort, or while sitting [5].
- The episode came with palpitations, chest pain, jerking of the limbs, or if your child wet themselves at the end [5].

## Common questions
**Is fainting dangerous?** In general, fainting is not caused by serious medical problems and does not need special testing [3]. It is not a disease [3]. However, you should seek urgent care if the warning signs listed above are present [5].

**Can my child feel anything before fainting?** Yes. Before fainting, your child may feel nausea, have blurred vision, belly pain, sweat, or hear ringing in their ears [1]. Recognizing these warning signs can help your child know what is about to happen and possibly avoid future fainting episodes [1].

**What should I do if my child faints?** Lay them down with their legs raised and head turned to the side [4]. After they recover, have them get up slowly, sitting first before standing [4].
