## What it is
Hand, foot and mouth disease is a common childhood illness that can also affect adults [4]. It is most common in children under 5 years old, but anyone can get it [2]. The illness is usually not serious, but it is very contagious [2]. It can spread quickly at schools and day care centers [2]. Most people get better on their own in 7 to 10 days [2][4]. Hand, foot and mouth disease is not the same as foot and mouth disease that affects farm animals [4].

Most children have mild symptoms for 7 to 10 days [2]. Symptoms may include:
- Fever [2]
- Sore throat [2]
- Painful mouth sores that blister [2]
- Rash commonly found on the hands and feet [2]

The illness is spread in coughs, sneezes, poo and the fluid in the blisters [3]. You can get it more than once [3]. You can start spreading it from a few days before you have any symptoms, but you're most likely to spread it to others in the first 5 days after symptoms start [3]. Complications from HFMD are rare [2][7].

## What you can do at home
Hand, foot and mouth disease usually gets better on its own in 7 to 10 days [6]. You cannot take antibiotics or other medicines to cure it [6]. To help the symptoms:
- Drink cool fluids to soothe the mouth and prevent dehydration, but avoid acidic drinks such as fruit juice [6]
- Eat soft foods like yoghurt and avoid hot, salty and spicy foods [6]
- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen to help ease a sore mouth or throat [6]
- Take over-the-counter medications to relieve fever and pain caused by mouth sores [9]
- Never give aspirin to children [9]
- Make sure your child drinks enough liquids to stay hydrated, as mouth sores can make it painful to swallow [9]

Ask a pharmacist for advice about treatments, such as mouth ulcer gels, sprays and mouthwashes, to relieve pain [1]. They can tell you which ones are suitable for children [1].

To reduce the risk of spreading hand, foot and mouth disease:
- Wash your hands often with soap and water, and children's hands too [3]
- Use tissues to trap germs when you cough or sneeze [3]
- Bin used tissues as quickly as possible [3]
- Do not share towels or household items like cups or cutlery [3]
- Wash soiled bedding and clothing on a hot wash [3]

Keep your child off school or nursery while they're feeling too unwell to go [10]. As soon as they're feeling better, they can go back to school or nursery [10]. There's no need to wait until all the blisters have healed [10]. Keeping your child away from other children for longer is unlikely to stop the illness spreading [10].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Non-urgent advice: See a GP if:
- Symptoms of hand, foot and mouth disease do not improve after 7 to 10 days [1]
- You're pregnant and get hand, foot and mouth disease [1]

Hand, foot and mouth disease can be spread to other people [1]. Check with your doctor's surgery before going [1]. They may suggest a phone consultation [1].

Urgent advice: Ask your doctor for an urgent appointment or get medical advice if:
- You or your child has a very high temperature, or feels hot, cold or shivery [1]
- You're worried about your child's hand, foot and mouth disease symptoms [1]
- Your child has hand, foot and mouth disease and is peeing less than usual (they may be becoming dehydrated) [1]

If you are pregnant, contact your doctor if you think you may have been exposed to HFMD [7]. Although there's usually no risk to the pregnancy or baby, it's best to avoid close contact with anyone who has hand, foot and mouth disease [5]. This is because getting hand, foot and mouth disease shortly before giving birth can mean your baby is born with a mild version of it [5]. Speak to a GP or your midwife if you have been in contact with someone with hand, foot and mouth disease [5].

## Common questions
**Can my child go back to school before the blisters heal?**
Yes. As soon as they're feeling better, they can go back to school or nursery [10]. There's no need to wait until all the blisters have healed [10].

**Can my child get hand, foot and mouth disease more than once?**
Yes, you can get it more than once [3].

**How long is my child contagious?**
You can start spreading it from a few days before you have any symptoms, but you're most likely to spread it to others in the first 5 days after symptoms start [3].
