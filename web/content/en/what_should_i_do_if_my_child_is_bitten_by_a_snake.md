## What it is

A bite from a venomous snake can be a serious medical emergency. Venom can cause severe paralysis that may stop a child breathing, bleeding problems that can lead to dangerous blood loss, kidney failure, and damage to the skin and tissue that can leave a permanent disability or lead to amputation [1].

Children may be affected more severely and more quickly than adults, because their body mass is smaller [1]. Most snake bites happen in tropical and subtropical countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America, but bites also happen elsewhere, including the UK [1][8].

The good news is that a highly effective treatment exists. High-quality snake antivenoms are the most effective treatment to prevent or reverse most of the venom's effects, and most deaths and serious consequences are preventable when antivenom is available [1].

## What you can do at home

- Treat every snake bite as an emergency and get medical help straight away, even if your child seems well at first [8].
- Do not drive yourself to the emergency department. Ask someone else to drive, or call your local emergency number for an ambulance [8].
- Keep your child as calm and still as you can while you wait for help.
- Take any medicines your child uses with you to the hospital [8].
- Do not go near the snake, or try to catch or kill it [10].
- Do not try to suck or cut the venom out of the bite [10].
- Do not tie anything tightly around the part of the body where the bite is [10].
- Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen, as they can make bleeding worse [10].
- If you are bitten abroad, contact the emergency medical services in the country you are in [10].

At the hospital, the bite will usually be cleaned and bandaged, and your child may be given an injection to help protect against tetanus [9]. If the snake was venomous, your child will be treated with antivenom given through a thin tube into a vein (a drip) [9]. You will usually need to stay in hospital for at least 24 hours [9].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Call your local emergency number or go to the emergency department immediately if you think your child, or anyone else, has been bitten by a snake [8]. Do not wait to see whether symptoms appear.

## Common questions

**Are all snake bites dangerous?**
Most snake bites in the UK are not serious, but every snake bite must be checked by a medical team as soon as possible [8]. Some snakes are venomous, meaning they release a harmful toxin through their bite, and these bites need antivenom treatment [9].

**Why are children at higher risk?**
Children may suffer more severe effects and can feel them more quickly than adults because of their smaller body mass [1].

**Is there a treatment?**
Yes. High-quality snake antivenoms are the most effective treatment to prevent or reverse most of the venom's effects, and they are on the WHO List of essential medicines [1]. They should be part of any primary health care package where snake bites occur [1].

**Why is antivenom sometimes hard to get?**
In some parts of the world, supply is a real problem. Poor data on how many bites occur makes it hard to estimate needs, and low demand has led some manufacturers to stop production or raise prices, so antivenom has become unaffordable or unavailable in some areas [4][7]. Weak regulation has also allowed inappropriate, untested or even fake antivenom products onto some markets [7].

**What is being done about it?**
WHO listed snakebite envenoming as a highest priority neglected tropical disease in 2017 and set a goal of a 50% reduction in mortality and disability caused by snakebite by 2030 [6]. Its strategy works on four fronts: empowering and engaging communities, ensuring safe and effective treatment, strengthening health systems, and increasing partnerships, coordination and resources [6].

**Can I give my child medicine for the pain?**
Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen after a snake bite, as they can make bleeding worse [10]. Ask the doctors treating your child what is safe to give.
