## What it is
Gastroenteritis is an infection of the digestive tract. It causes more frequent, soft or liquid stools. Stools may have small amounts of blood or mucus. Your child may also have belly pain, vomiting, and fever [3]. Vomiting is the forceful expulsion of stomach contents through the mouth. It is often caused by a gastrointestinal issue [6]. Gastroenteritis is usually not serious in healthy people, but it can sometimes cause dehydration or more severe symptoms [10]. Causes include viruses, bacteria, parasites, chemicals, and reactions to certain medications or foods. Viral gastroenteritis is the most common type [10]. The main risk is dehydration from fluid loss through diarrhea and vomiting [3]. The danger of dehydration is greater the younger the child is [2].

## What you can do at home
The most important goal is to prevent dehydration [3]. Oral rehydration solutions contain water, sugar, and salts in a precise balance to replace losses [3]. Give the amount you were told after each loose stool or vomit [3]. Offer small doses frequently so they are better tolerated [3].

- If your child vomits, wait 20 minutes, then give oral rehydration solution, 2-5 ml every 5 minutes for one hour [5].
- Watch how your child accepts the fluids. If tolerated well, you can gradually increase the frequency [5].
- Do not use homemade solutions or commercial soft drinks to replace losses [2].
- Do not give your child any medication for diarrhea or vomiting without consulting a pediatrician [2].

Once your child tolerates fluids well, you can offer food without forcing it, in small amounts [7]. No fasting is needed; children should keep eating an almost normal diet unless a doctor says otherwise [1]. Give smaller, more frequent portions, respecting your child's appetite [1].

- For babies, use their usual milk, whether breast milk or formula [7].
- For older children, gradually reintroduce their normal diet. Cereals, chicken, turkey, fruits, and vegetables are usually well tolerated [7].
- Do not recommend a bland diet [7].
- Avoid fatty foods, sweets, and juices [1]. Avoid chocolate, candy, legumes, flatulent vegetables, highly seasoned foods, very fatty foods, fried foods, and sauces [7].
- Dairy products do not need to be avoided. Continue normal breastfeeding, even more often if your child asks [1].
- Avoid sports drinks because they have too much sugar and inadequate electrolyte levels for a child with diarrhea [1].
- Avoid soft drinks and juices rich in sugars at any age [7].

Frequent hand washing for the patient and caregivers is essential to prevent transmission. Alcohol-based hand solution is an alternative [2]. Keep sick children out of school, daycare, or pools until vomiting has stopped [7]. Symptoms usually resolve on their own in less than one week [7].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Seek care if your child has any of these signs [1][2][8]:

- Vomits a lot, cannot tolerate solids or liquids, or vomits even when not taking anything [1].
- Vomiting is greenish, contains blood, or looks like brown coffee grounds [1].
- Very thirsty, cries without tears, urinates little, or has sunken eyes [2].
- Marked irritability, sleepiness, or decreased responsiveness [2].
- Stools are very liquid, abundant, and frequent, so the child cannot drink at the same rate as the losses [2].
- Refuses to eat or drink [2].
- Persistent or intense abdominal pain, or stools with a lot of blood [2].
- Change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy [8].
- Diarrhea lasting more than one day [8].
- Any fever in babies, or high fever in older children [8].
- Frequent loose stools or frequent vomiting [8].
- Severe pain in the abdomen or rectum [8].
- Black, tarry stools, or stools containing blood or pus [8].
- Signs of dehydration, such as thirst, dry mouth, urinating less than usual, or no wet diapers for 3 hours or more, and no tears when crying [8].

## Common questions
**Can my child keep eating dairy products?**
Yes. Dairy products do not need to be avoided. Continue normal breastfeeding, even more often if your child asks [1].

**Can I give my child sports drinks?**
No. Sports drinks have too much sugar and inadequate electrolyte levels for a child with diarrhea [1].

**Can I give my child medication for diarrhea or vomiting?**
Do not give any medication for diarrhea or vomiting without consulting a pediatrician [2].

**When can my child go back to school or daycare?**
Keep sick children out of school, daycare, or pools until vomiting has stopped [7].
