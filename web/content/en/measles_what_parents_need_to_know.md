## What it is
Measles is an infection caused by a virus. It spreads very easily from person to person [1][3]. The virus spreads when an infected person breathes, coughs, or sneezes [3][5]. It can cause serious problems and even death in some people [2][3]. Measles can affect anyone but is most common in children [3].

Symptoms usually begin 7–14 days after exposure [4]. Early symptoms last 4–7 days and include:
- Fever [1][2][4]
- Runny or blocked nose [1][2][4]
- Cough [1][2][4]
- Red, sore, watery eyes (conjunctivitis) [1][2][4]
- Feeling achy and run down [1]
- Small white spots inside the cheeks and on the back of the lips [1][2][4]

A blotchy red rash usually appears 3–5 days after the first symptoms [1][4]. It often starts on the face and upper neck, then spreads downward over about 3 days to the trunk, arms, legs, and feet [1][4]. The rash usually lasts 4–8 days before fading [4].

Measles can lead to complications, especially in children under 5 years and adults over 30 [4]. Complications can include blindness, encephalitis (brain swelling), severe diarrhoea, ear infections, and pneumonia [4]. Measles also weakens the immune system, leaving children vulnerable to other infections [4]. If a woman catches measles during pregnancy, it can be dangerous for the mother and may cause miscarriage or premature birth with low birth weight [4].

There is no treatment for measles itself [1]. The MMR or MMRV vaccine is the best way to prevent it [1][2][3][8]. Children should receive two doses of the vaccine [7]. The first dose is usually given at 9 months in countries where measles is common, or 12–15 months in other countries. A second dose is given later in childhood, usually at 15–18 months [7].

## What you can do at home
Measles usually starts to get better in about a week [10]. After seeing a GP, you can help ease symptoms and reduce spread [10]. It can help to:
- Rest and drink plenty of fluids, such as water, to avoid dehydration [10]
- Give your child paracetamol for children or ibuprofen for children if they are distressed or uncomfortable – check the packaging or leaflet to make sure the medicine is suitable, or ask a pharmacist or GP [10]
- Adults can take paracetamol or ibuprofen for a high temperature [10]
- Use cotton wool soaked in water that has been boiled and allowed to cool to gently remove crusts from the eyes [10]

To reduce the risk of spreading measles:
- Open windows and doors to help reduce spread – only open the top window or use safety catches if you have young children [5]
- Wash your hands often with soap and warm water [5]
- Use tissues when you cough or sneeze and throw them in the bin immediately [5]
- Do not share cutlery, cups, towels, clothes, or bedding [5]

Stay off nursery, school, or work for at least 4 days from when the rash first appears [10]. Also try to avoid close contact with babies, anyone who is pregnant, or anyone with a weakened immune system [10].

You are infectious from when you first have symptoms (around 4 days before the rash appears) until 4 days after you get the rash [5].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Ask your doctor for an urgent appointment or get medical advice if [6]:
- You think you or your child may have measles [6]
- Your child is under 1 year old and has come into contact with someone who has measles [6]
- You have been in close contact with someone who has measles and you are pregnant or have a weakened immune system [6]
- You or your child have not had measles or 2 doses of the MMRV or MMR vaccine and have been in close contact with someone with measles [6]
- You or your child have a high temperature that has not come down after taking paracetamol or ibuprofen [6]
- You or your child have difficulty breathing – you may feel more short of breath than usual [6]
- Your baby or young child is not feeding well, or taking less feeds or fluids than usual [6]
- You or your child are peeing less than usual (or your baby has fewer wet nappies) [6]
- You or your child feels very unwell, or you are worried something is seriously wrong [6]

Measles can spread easily to others. Call your doctor's surgery before you go in. They may suggest talking over the phone or arrange for you to come to a part of the surgery where you are separated from others [6].

## Common questions
**Is it measles if my child has been vaccinated?**
It is unlikely to be measles if your child has had both doses of the MMRV or MMR vaccine, or has had measles before [6].

**What is the difference between measles and German measles?**
"German measles," also known as rubella, is a completely different illness [1].

**How long does measles last?**
Measles usually starts to get better in about a week [10]. The rash typically lasts 4–8 days before fading [4].
