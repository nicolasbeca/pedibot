## What it is

A faint, also called a syncope, is a sudden and short loss of consciousness [2]. It usually happens when not enough blood or oxygen reaches the brain [2]. According to the SEUP, fainting is more common in older people [6].

Some situations make it more likely: extreme heat, pain, fear, nervousness or anxiety, standing still in the same spot for a long time, or getting up suddenly from lying down [2]. Less common causes include low blood sugar in children with diabetes, some poisonings, heart disease and dehydration [2].

Before fainting, a child may feel sick, see blurred vision, have belly pain, sweat, or hear ringing in the ears [1]. These warning signs can help a child notice what is coming and avoid another faint [1]. The child turns pale, stops responding and loses strength, and may fall to the ground [1]. Sometimes there are two or three jerks of the arms and legs when consciousness is lost [1].

Fainting is usually nothing to worry about, but it can sometimes be a sign of a serious problem [6]. In general, faints are not caused by important medical problems and do not need special tests [3]. They are not a disease [3].

## What you can do at home

- When your child starts to feel unwell, they should sit or lie down straight away to avoid the faint and the fall [4]. Changing position is often enough to treat it [4]. It helps to breathe slowly and deeply [4].
- If your child loses consciousness, lie them down with the legs raised and the head turned to the side so they breathe better [4].
- When the discomfort and weakness have passed, they can get up slowly. If lying down, they should sit first and stand up after a few minutes [4].
- After a period of rest, children usually recover quickly, within a couple of minutes [4].
- If you know the situations that cause the faints, avoid or change them. For example, do not get up suddenly from sitting or lying [3].
- If your child tends to feel faint when blood is taken, tell the nurse beforehand so they can lie down during the procedure [3].
- Children who tend to faint should drink more fluids, especially when they do physical exercise [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Go to the emergency department or call your local emergency number if:

- Your child takes more than 3 to 5 minutes to regain consciousness. Place them in the recovery position and call the emergency services [5].
- The faint happened during physical exercise, during effort, or while your child was sitting [5].
- The episode came with palpitations, chest pain, jerking of the arms or legs, or your child wet themselves at the end [5].

Also see your doctor if your child faints, so you can find out why it happened [6].

## Common questions

**Does a faint mean something is wrong with my child's heart?**
Heart problems are one possible but less frequent cause of fainting [2]. If the faint happened during exercise or effort, or came with chest pain or palpitations, seek urgent care [5].

**How long does it take to recover?**
After a period of rest, children usually recover quickly, in a couple of minutes [4]. When someone faints, make sure the airway is clear and check for breathing, and the person should stay lying down for 10 to 15 minutes [6].

**Can fainting be prevented?**
Yes. Avoid the situations that trigger it, get up slowly, drink more fluids, especially during exercise, and warn the nurse before blood tests if your child tends to feel faint [3].

**Do faints need special tests?**
In general, faints are not caused by important medical problems and do not need any special study [3]. Still, it is important to see your doctor to find out why it happened [6].
