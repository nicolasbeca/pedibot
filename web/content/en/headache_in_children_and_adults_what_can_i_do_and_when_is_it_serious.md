## What it is

Most headaches go away on their own and are not a sign of something more serious [1]. A headache can last 30 minutes, several hours, or sometimes several days [1]. Almost everyone has had a headache, and it is the most common form of pain [3]. The most common type is a tension headache, caused by tight muscles in the shoulders, neck, scalp and jaw [3]. Tension headaches are often linked to stress, depression or anxiety, and you are more likely to get them if you work too much, do not get enough sleep, miss meals, or drink alcohol [3]. Other common types include migraines, cluster headaches and sinus headaches [3]. Common triggers include having a cold or flu, stress, drinking too much alcohol, bad posture, eyesight problems, not eating regular meals, not drinking enough fluids, taking too many painkillers, and hormone changes such as having your period or going through menopause [2]. Keeping a headache diary can help you work out what triggers your headaches [2].

## What you can do at home

- Drink plenty of water [1].
- Get plenty of rest if you also have a cold or the flu [1].
- Try to relax, because stress can make headaches worse [1].
- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen [1]. Ask your pharmacist or doctor about the right dose for your child's age and weight, or use the dose calculator.
- Stay at home and avoid contact with other people if you also have a high temperature or do not feel well enough to do your normal activities [1].
- Do not skip meals, even if you do not feel like eating [4].
- Do not sleep more than you usually would, as it can make the headache worse [4].
- Do not strain your eyes for a long time, for example by looking at a screen [4].
- Do not drink alcohol [4].

Most people can feel much better by making lifestyle changes, learning ways to relax and taking pain relievers [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

See your doctor if you have tried treating a headache yourself and it is not getting better or is getting worse, or if you regularly get headaches [5].

Get urgent medical advice the same day if you or a child has a headache and:

- vision or eye problems [5]
- a headache triggered or made worse by coughing, sneezing, bending down or exercising [5]
- being sick (vomiting) [5]
- in a child, a headache that is getting worse or wakes them at night [5]
- jaw pain when eating, or a sore or tender scalp [5]
- any other symptoms you are worried about [5]

Get emergency help immediately if you or a child has a headache and:

- has had a seizure (fit) [5]
- has numbness or weakness in the body or face [5]
- it started suddenly and is extremely painful [5]
- has had a head injury within the last 3 months, for example from a fall or accident [5]
- is finding it difficult to speak, balance, walk or remember things [5]
- is drowsy or confused [5]
- has loss of vision [5]
- has a rash that does not fade when a glass is rolled over it [5]
- has a very high temperature, a stiff neck, or bright lights are bothering them [5]

Do not drive yourself to the emergency department. Ask someone to drive you or call your local emergency number for an ambulance, and bring any medicines you take with you [5]. Get medical help right away if a headache follows a blow to the head, or comes with a stiff neck, fever, confusion, loss of consciousness, or pain in the eye or ear [3]. Let your doctor know if you have sudden, severe headaches [3].

## Common questions

**Do all headaches need a doctor?**
No. Not all headaches require a doctor's attention, but sometimes headaches warn of a more serious disorder [3].

**Can I take too many painkillers?**
Yes. Taking too many painkillers is itself a common cause of headaches [2].

**What if my child is under 5 and I need advice?**
Ask your doctor or your local health advice service for advice about a child under 5 years old [5].

**What if someone faints with their headache?**
Fainting is a temporary loss of consciousness, and it usually happens when blood pressure drops suddenly, reducing blood flow to the brain [6]. When someone faints, make sure the airway is clear and check for breathing, and the person should stay lying down for 10 to 15 minutes [6]. Most people recover completely, but if you faint it is important to see your doctor and find out why it happened [6].
