## What it is

Weaning means introducing your baby to solid foods alongside breast milk or first infant formula. It should start when your baby is around 6 months old [2]. Before this age, breast milk or first infant formula provides all the energy and nutrients your baby needs [5]. Waiting until around 6 months gives your baby time to develop the skills needed to cope with solid foods [5].

There are 3 clear signs that show your baby is ready for solid foods. These signs appear together from around 6 months [4]. Your baby will be able to:
- Stay in a sitting position and hold their head steady [4]
- Co-ordinate their eyes, hands and mouth so they can look at food, pick it up and put it in their mouth by themselves [4]
- Swallow food rather than spit it back out [4]

Some behaviours are not signs of readiness. Chewing fists, waking up more at night, or wanting extra milk feeds are normal baby behaviours [4]. Starting solid foods will not make your baby more likely to sleep through the night [4].

## What you can do at home

At the beginning, how much your baby eats matters less than getting them used to the idea of eating [2]. They will still get most of their energy and nutrients from breast milk or first infant formula [8].

Start by offering small amounts of food, just a few pieces or teaspoons [8]. Offer food before their usual milk feed, but do not wait until they are too hungry [8]. Pick a time when you both feel relaxed [8].

Let your baby go at their own pace [8]. Stop when they show signs they have had enough, such as closing their mouth firmly or turning their head away [10]. Do not force your baby to eat [10].

Keep offering different foods, even ones your baby has rejected [8]. It can take 10 tries or more before your baby accepts a new food or texture [8]. Give lots of encouragement and praise [10].

Let your baby touch and hold food, and feed themselves with their fingers as soon as they show interest [10]. If using a spoon, wait for your baby to open their mouth before offering food [10]. Keep distractions like television or phones away during mealtimes [10]. Sit together for family meals, as babies copy their parents [10].

Move from puréed foods to mashed, lumpy or finger foods as soon as your baby can manage them [6]. Some babies start with mashed or finger foods directly [6]. Others need smooth foods at first, but keep offering lumpy textures [6].

Introduce a cup from around 6 months and offer sips of water with meals [8]. Use an open cup or a free-flow cup without a valve [8].

Safety and hygiene steps:
- Always wash your hands before preparing food and keep surfaces clean [9]
- Cool hot food and test it before giving it to your baby [9]
- Wash and peel fruit and raw vegetables [9]
- Avoid hard foods like whole nuts, raw carrot or apple [9]
- Remove hard pips and stones from fruit, and bones from meat or fish [9]
- Cut small round foods like grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters [9]
- Always stay with your baby when they are eating in case they choke [9]

Do not add sugar or salt to your baby's food or cooking water [10]. Babies should not eat salty foods as it is not good for their kidneys, and sugar can cause tooth decay [10]. Do not give whole cows', goats' or sheep's milk as a main drink until your baby is 1 year old [8].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

If your baby was born prematurely, ask your health visitor or GP for advice on when to start solid foods [2].

Gagging is different from choking. Your baby may gag when learning to eat solid foods [9]. Signs of gagging include watering eyes, pushing the tongue forward, or retching to bring food forward [9]. If you think your child is choking, seek immediate help [9].

## Common questions

**What is baby-led weaning?**
Baby-led weaning means giving your baby only finger foods and letting them feed themselves from the start, instead of spoon-feeding purées [1]. Some parents prefer this, others do a combination [1]. There is no right or wrong way [1]. There is no more risk of choking when babies feed themselves than when fed with a spoon [1].

**How many meals does my baby need at first?**
Babies do not need 3 meals a day when they first start solid foods [8]. They have tiny tummies, so start with small amounts [8]. Breast milk or infant formula should be their main drink during the first year [8].

**What if my baby rejects a food?**
Keep offering different foods, even ones your baby has already rejected [8]. It can take 10 tries or more before your baby accepts a new food or texture [8]. There will be days when they eat more, some when they eat less, and days when they reject everything [10]. This is perfectly normal [10].
