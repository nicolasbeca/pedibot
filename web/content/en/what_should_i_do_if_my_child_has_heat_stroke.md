## What it is
Heat stroke is a serious condition that can affect children, especially when temperatures are high. It happens when the body cannot control its temperature effectively, leading to a dangerous rise in body temperature [3]. This can occur due to prolonged exposure to the sun or hot environments, intense physical activity in warm climates, not drinking enough fluids, or wearing clothes that prevent sweating [3].

Symptoms include hot, red, and dry skin (without sweating in severe cases), high fever (above 39°C), dizziness, confusion or disorientation, intense headache, nausea or vomiting, and loss of consciousness in severe cases [3]. In some situations, body temperature can rise above 41°C (106°F) in minutes [5]. Heat stroke can cause damage to vital organs and put a child's life at risk if not treated immediately [2].

## What you can do at home
If your child has been exposed to high temperatures or done intense exercise in a warm place and feels unwell, act quickly [1].

- Move the child to a cool, shaded place immediately [1][2].
- Remove excess clothing [1][2].
- Apply cool, wet cloths to the forehead, neck, and armpits [2].
- Offer fresh water in small sips [2].
- Use fans to help lower body temperature [1][2].

If treated quickly, most children recover without complications [2]. Prevention is also key. Keep children hydrated with water and natural juices, avoid direct sun exposure between 11:00 and 17:00, dress them in light, light-colored clothing, and never leave children inside cars [1]. Encourage rest in cool, ventilated places [1].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Go to the emergency department if your child has been exposed to high temperatures or done intense exercise in a warm environment and shows any of these signs [4]:

- Fever above 39°C that does not go down with home measures [4].
- Loss of consciousness or seizures [4].
- Confusion, difficulty speaking, or difficulty moving [4].
- Persistent vomiting and cannot drink fluids [4].

Also seek immediate medical help if you see signs like dry skin, rapid and strong pulse, dizziness, nausea, or confusion [5]. These can indicate heat stroke, which is life-threatening [5].

## Common questions
**Can I give my child fever medicine for heat stroke?**
The sources do not mention using fever medication for heat stroke. The recommended home measures are cooling the child and offering fluids [2]. If fever is above 39°C and does not improve with these measures, seek emergency care [4].

**How long does it take to recover from heat stroke?**
If treated quickly, most children recover without complications [2]. The sources do not give a specific recovery time.

**Is heat stroke the same as heat exhaustion?**
No. Heat exhaustion can happen after several days in high heat and includes heavy sweating, rapid breathing, and a weak, fast pulse [5]. If not treated, it can lead to heat stroke, which is more severe and life-threatening [5].
