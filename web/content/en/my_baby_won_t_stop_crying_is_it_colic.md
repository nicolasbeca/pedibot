## What it is
Colic is a benign condition, not a serious illness [1]. It causes episodes of prolonged crying in healthy babies, often repeating almost daily, especially in the late afternoon and evening [4]. Episodes can last from minutes to hours [4]. The cause is unknown, but possible explanations include an immature digestive system, constipation, changes in gut bacteria, or trouble passing gas [4].

Crying episodes usually start around 15 days of life, peak at 5-6 weeks, and rarely last beyond the fourth month [1]. During an episode, your baby may pull their legs toward their belly, clench their fists, and have a red face from effort [2]. The crying is paroxysmal (has a clear start and end), intense and high-pitched, and inconsolable [2]. When the crying stops, your baby returns to a normal appearance [2].

## What you can do at home
- Try to stay calm, as this is a harmless and temporary process [5].
- Go to comfort your baby and rule out other causes of crying: hunger, thirst, a wet diaper, being too hot or too cold [5].
- Keep the environment calm and relaxed, try not to pass anxiety to your baby, and ask for help so you can take a break from caregiving [5].
- Abdominal massages or rides in a stroller or car may help [5].

Colic is slightly less common in breastfed babies [6]. Cow's milk protein allergy may trigger colic in a small number of cases, but talk to your pediatrician before making any feeding changes [6].

Do not give your baby any product (from a pharmacy, parapharmacy, or herbalist) or change formula without consulting your pediatrician [7]. Many commercial products, herbal remedies, and other "cures" have effectiveness that is more apparent than real, similar to a placebo [7]. Some natural plants, like star anise, can be dangerous [7]. Some breastfed babies may improve with probiotics, but there are no studies showing clear scientific evidence, because colic eventually disappears on its own [7]. Currently, no treatment has proven effectiveness [7]. It is normal for healthy babies to cry for two hours or more a day, even without colic [7].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Go to urgent care if any of the following occur [3]:
- Your baby does not return to a normal state between crying episodes.
- The crying is accompanied by paleness, sweating, listlessness, or refusal to feed.
- Your baby has a fever, vomits, or has blood in their stool.

## Common questions
**How long will the colic last?**
Crying episodes usually start around 15 days of life, peak at 5-6 weeks, and rarely last beyond the fourth month [1]. Colic is a benign condition and eventually disappears on its own [7].

**Is my baby in pain?**
During episodes, babies often pull their legs up, clench their fists, and have a red face from effort [2]. When the crying stops, they return to a normal appearance [2]. Colic is not a serious illness [1].

**Should I change the formula or give medicine?**
Do not give any product or change formula without consulting your pediatrician [7]. No treatment has proven effectiveness [7]. Talk to your doctor before any feeding changes, as cow's milk protein allergy may be a trigger in a small number of cases [6].
