## What it is
Being a teenager is hard. Teens face stress to be liked, do well in school, get along with family, and make big decisions [2]. Worrying about these pressures is normal [2]. But feeling very sad, hopeless, or worthless could be warning signs of a mental health problem [2]. Mental health problems are real, painful, and sometimes severe [2].

Globally, one in seven 10–19-year-olds experiences a mental disorder [3]. Anxiety disorders are the most common emotional disorder in this age group [1]. Depression and anxiety share some symptoms, including rapid and unexpected changes in mood [1]. These conditions can profoundly affect school attendance and schoolwork [1]. Social withdrawal can worsen isolation and loneliness [1]. Depression can lead to suicide [1]. Suicide is the third leading cause of death among those aged 15–29 years [3].

Eating disorders, such as anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, commonly emerge during adolescence [4]. They involve abnormal eating behavior and preoccupation with food, often with concerns about body weight and shape [4]. Girls are more commonly affected than boys [4]. Eating disorders often co-exist with depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders [4]. They are associated with suicide [4]. Anorexia nervosa has higher mortality than any other mental disorder [4].

Conditions with psychosis symptoms most commonly emerge in late adolescence or early adulthood [10]. Symptoms can include hallucinations or delusions [10]. These can impair a teen’s ability to participate in daily life and education [10].

## What you can do at home
Mental health problems can be treated [2]. To find help, talk to your teen’s parents, school counselor, or health care provider [2]. Adolescence is a crucial period for developing social and emotional habits important for mental well-being [8]. These habits include:
- Adopting healthy sleep patterns [8]
- Exercising regularly [8]
- Developing coping, problem-solving, and interpersonal skills [8]
- Learning to manage emotions [8]

Protective and supportive environments in the family, at school, and in the wider community are important [8]. Mental health promotion aims to strengthen a teen’s ability to regulate emotions, build resilience for managing difficult situations, and promote supportive social networks [9]. Multiple factors affect mental health, and the more risk factors a teen is exposed to, the greater the potential impact [8]. Risk factors include violence, harsh parenting, and severe socioeconomic problems [8]. Addressing adolescent mental health conditions is crucial, and prioritizing non-pharmacological approaches is key [7].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
You might need help if your teen shows these signs [2]:
- Often feels very angry or very worried [2]
- Feels grief for a long time after a loss or death [2]
- Thinks their mind is controlled or out of control [2]
- Uses alcohol or drugs [2]
- Exercises, diets, and/or binge-eats obsessively [2]
- Hurts other people or destroys property [2]
- Does reckless things that could harm them or others [2]
- Feels depressed (sad and hopeless) [2]

Feeling very sad, hopeless, or worthless could be warning signs of a mental health problem [2]. Depression can lead to suicide [1]. Eating disorders are associated with suicide [4]. Seek help promptly if you notice these signs.

## Common questions
**Is it normal for teens to have mood swings?**
Depression and anxiety share some symptoms, including rapid and unexpected changes in mood [1]. Worrying about normal pressures is normal [2]. But feeling very sad, hopeless, or worthless could be a warning sign [2].

**Are mental health problems in teens common?**
Yes. Globally, one in seven 10–19-year-olds experiences a mental disorder [3]. Anxiety disorders are estimated in 4.1% of 10–14-year-olds and 5.3% of 15–19-year-olds [1]. Depression is estimated in 1.3% of 10–14-year-olds and 3.4% of 15–19-year-olds [1].

**Can mental health problems affect my teen’s future?**
Yes. The consequences of failing to address adolescent mental health conditions extend to adulthood, impairing both physical and mental health and limiting opportunities to lead fulfilling lives as adults [3].
