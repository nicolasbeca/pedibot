## What it is

Breath-holding spells are episodes that are not serious or dangerous. They happen when a child, after a strong fright, a fall, or because of anger, cries or tries to cry and stops breathing for a few seconds, turning purple or pale. Usually the child then bursts into tears. Sometimes the child may faint or make jerking movements [1]. These spells are common in children between 6 months and 5 years old [1].

The duration is short, usually less than 1 minute, and the child recovers quickly. Breath-holding spells are benign and cause NO DAMAGE to the child at that moment, nor will they cause complications in the future [2].

## What you can do at home

- When a spell happens, it is normal to feel anxious and worried, but it is important to try to STAY CALM, as it will resolve on its own without doing anything [4].
- Do not shake, agitate, give mouth-to-mouth, resuscitate, or shout at the child, as this could even make the situation worse [4].
- Breath-holding spells cannot be prevented. They will disappear before the age of 5 [3].
- If possible, record the episode on video so you can show it to your pediatrician [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Go to an emergency department if any of the following happens [3]:

- It is the first time it occurs.
- The child is younger than 6 months.
- The spell lasts more than 1-2 minutes.
- The child faints.
- When the child recovers, he or she is very drowsy.
- The child makes abnormal movements.

If the spells are very frequent and/or if you have doubts, you can (and should) consult your pediatrician [3].

## Common questions

**Are breath-holding spells dangerous?**
No. They are benign and do not cause any damage at the moment, nor will they cause complications in the future [2].

**How long do breath-holding spells last?**
They are usually short, lasting less than 1 minute, and the child recovers quickly [2].

**Can I prevent breath-holding spells?**
No, they cannot be prevented. They will disappear before the age of 5 [3].

**What should I do during a spell?**
Stay calm. The spell will resolve on its own in a few seconds without you doing anything [4]. Do not shake the child, give mouth-to-mouth, or shout [4].

**My child turned blue or pale. Is that normal?**
Yes. During a spell, after a strong fright, a fall, or because of anger, the child cries or tries to cry and stops breathing for a few seconds, turning purple or pale [1].
