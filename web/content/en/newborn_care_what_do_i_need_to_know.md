## What it is
A healthy newborn is a baby born at term (37 weeks or more) whose history, physical exam, and adaptation to life outside the womb are normal [8]. In the first hours after birth, your baby will be checked for breathing, color, heart rate, and any signs of infection or metabolic problems [10]. Most babies born by vaginal delivery who appear healthy can be placed directly on their mother's chest right away, if the mother wishes [3]. This early skin-to-skin contact helps prevent heat loss, supports breastfeeding, and strengthens the bond between mother and baby [3]. Your baby should stay in the room with you as much as possible during the hospital stay, so you can learn to respond to his or her needs [1].

## What you can do at home
- **Sleep position:** Always place your baby on his or her back (supine) or side in a crib with a firm mattress and no pillow. Never place a baby on his or her stomach (prone), unless a doctor says it is medically necessary [1][5].
- **Bathing:** A daily bath is fine, using warm water [1]. However, a full bath is not usually necessary for a newborn [4][5]. You can clean the diaper area with a soft sponge and water or a mild soap at each diaper change [4][5]. Do not bathe a newborn in the delivery room; just dry him or her with warm towels [7].
- **Cord care:** Wash the umbilical cord with soap and water, then dry it well. Do this each time it gets dirty, such as at diaper changes. The cord usually falls off between 5 and 15 days of life. Keep cleaning the area the same way until it is fully dry [1]. Antiseptics like chlorhexidine 4% or 70% alcohol may be used, but avoid iodine-based antiseptics [2][9].
- **Dressing:** A cotton shirt or a simple sleepsuit without buttons, plus a diaper, is usually enough. Crib bedding should be soft, without frills or rough seams [5].
- **Feeding:** Breast milk is the preferred food if there is no medical reason against it. Start breastfeeding as soon as possible after birth. Avoid giving water, oral rehydration solutions, or formula unless medically needed [5]. Mothers who choose formula should receive the same support and not feel guilty [1].
- **Daily checks:** Watch your baby's heart rate and breathing. Note how often your baby passes urine and stool [1]. Weighing daily is not necessary for healthy newborns; weighing at discharge or on day 3-4 is enough to check the normal weight loss of 4-7% (not more than 10-12%) [1].
- **Visitors:** Healthy visitors, including siblings, do not need to be restricted. But agree with the mother on a number of people that will not interfere with rest and baby care [1].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Call a doctor or go to the emergency department if your baby shows any of these warning signs:
- Difficulty breathing or bluish skin (cyanosis) [4]
- Sweating or a heart murmur [4]
- Low body temperature (hypothermia) [4]
- Trembling [4]
- Floppy (hypotonia) or stiff (hypertonia) muscles [4]
- Lethargy or unusual irritability [4]

Also seek medical advice if your baby has risk factors for infection, such as:
- Rupture of membranes more than 18 hours before birth [4]
- Signs of infection in the mother, like fever during labor (38.5°C or higher), foul-smelling vaginal discharge, or foul-smelling amniotic fluid [4]
- Untreated urinary infection in the mother during the third trimester [4]
- A positive test for group B streptococcus in the mother that was not properly treated during labor [4]

Babies with these risk factors should be observed in the hospital for at least 48 hours, even if they appear healthy [4].

## Common questions
**Do I need to bathe my baby every day?**
No. A daily bath is acceptable, but it is not necessary. You can clean the diaper area with a sponge and water or mild soap at each diaper change [4][5][6]. Avoid using soap every day if you do bathe daily [6].

**When will the umbilical cord fall off?**
The cord usually falls off between 5 and 15 days of life. Keep the area clean and dry until the wound is fully healed [1].

**Can I bathe my baby before the cord falls off?**
Yes, you can bathe your baby even if the cord has not fallen off yet [6]. Just dry the cord well afterward [1].

**What position should my baby sleep in?**
Always place your baby on the back or side, on a firm mattress without a pillow. Never place a baby on the stomach unless a doctor says it is medically necessary [1][5].
