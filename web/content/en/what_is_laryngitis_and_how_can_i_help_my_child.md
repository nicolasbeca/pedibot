## What it is
Laryngitis, also called croup, is an inflammation of the larynx, the part of the throat where the vocal cords are [3]. It is caused by different viruses and is most common in children between 6 months and 6 years old, especially in autumn and early winter [3]. It often starts like a common cold with mucus and congestion [1]. Then, suddenly, usually at night, a child develops a "barking" cough that sounds like a dog or a seal [1]. The cough can come with hoarseness and may get worse if the child is nervous or crying [1]. When air passes through the inflamed larynx, you may hear a noise called "stridor" [3]. There may also be fever and high-pitched sounds when breathing [6]. Symptoms generally get worse at night and last between three and five days [6]. Most cases of viral croup are mild and can be treated at home [6]. Complications are rare [2].

## What you can do at home
- Keep the air moist. Put containers of water on radiators to avoid a dry environment [5].
- Humidity can help. Hold your child in the bathroom with hot water running, or let them breathe cool, fresh air while dressed warmly, by opening a window or going out on a terrace [5].
- If your child has a fever, give the fever reducer your pediatrician usually recommends [5].
- It is normal for your child to have little appetite. Do not force them to eat. Offer fluids [5].
- Let your child rest at home for a few days. They often cough a lot at night and do not sleep well [2].
- Do not give antibiotics. They do not help with viral infections [4][7].
- Do not give cough syrups or suppositories without asking your pediatrician first [4].
- Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers [7].
- Talk to your child's doctor before giving any cold or cough medicine [7].

Symptoms usually resolve in a few days but can last up to a week [2]. More severe cases are treated with oral or nebulized anti-inflammatories [2].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Go to the emergency department if your child:

- Makes noise when breathing in (stridor), even when calm [4].
- Has worsening breathing difficulty: breathes faster and faster, ribs are visible, abdomen moves a lot, chest sinks in, or neck stretches [4].
- Has trouble swallowing or drools a lot [4].

Also seek medical help if you are worried about your child's breathing [6]. Watch your child's breathing, color, and general condition at home [2]. Call your doctor if your child has problems breathing, rapid breathing, dehydration, fever lasting more than 4 days, symptoms lasting more than 10 days without improvement, or symptoms that improve then return or worsen [7].

## Common questions
**Can my child go to school?**
It is recommended that children rest at home for a few days, as they often cough a lot at night and do not sleep well [2]. Generally, if the child is well and has no fever, they can attend school [10]. If they have fever, it is recommended that 24 hours pass without fever before returning [10].

**Do antibiotics help?**
No. Laryngitis is caused by viruses, and antibiotics do not help with viral infections [4][7].

**How long does it last?**
Symptoms usually resolve in a few days but can persist up to a week [2]. Most cases are mild and benign [4].
