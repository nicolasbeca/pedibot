## What it is

Vaccines protect your child from serious diseases that can harm or kill infants, children, and adults [8]. Babies are born with immune systems that can fight most germs, but some serious diseases are too much for them to handle [8]. Vaccines strengthen their immune system so they can build immunity without getting sick [8]. For a few vaccines, getting vaccinated gives a better immune response than getting the disease would [8].

Your child will get vaccines during well-child visits, according to a vaccine schedule [1]. This schedule lists which vaccines are recommended, how many doses are needed, and at what age they should be given [1]. In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) publishes the vaccine schedule [1]. Following the schedule gives your child protection at the right time, allowing their body to build immunity before being exposed to these diseases [1].

Vaccinating your child also protects others [8]. When enough people are vaccinated, it is harder for a disease to spread, which is called "community immunity" [8]. This is especially important for people who cannot get vaccines, such as those with weakened immune systems, allergies to vaccine ingredients, or newborn babies who are too young [8].

## What you can do at home

- Keep your child's vaccination appointments at their well-child visits [1].
- If you think your child missed a vaccine, contact your GP surgery to catch up [2][5].
- If you missed or cannot attend a vaccination appointment, your GP surgery can book or rearrange it [5].
- It is best to have vaccines on time, but you can still catch up on most vaccines if you miss them [5].

Do not worry about vaccines overloading your child's immune system. Every day, a healthy child's immune system fights off thousands of germs [6]. Vaccines contain weakened or dead germs, so even several vaccines in one day expose your child to a tiny amount of germs compared to what they encounter daily [6].

Some vaccines are only available for groups who need extra protection, such as children with long-term health conditions or weakened immune systems [4][7]. Ask your doctor if your child qualifies for any extra vaccines.

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

- If you think your child has missed any vaccinations, speak to your GP surgery [5].
- If your child has a vaccination appointment but you missed it or cannot attend, contact your GP surgery to rearrange [5].
- If your child is starting college or university, make sure they have had the MenACWY vaccine, 2 doses of MMR, and the HPV vaccine [5]. You can ask your GP for these until your 25th birthday [5].

## Common questions

**Can vaccines overload my child's immune system?**
No. Vaccines do not overload the immune system [6]. A healthy child's immune system successfully fights off thousands of germs every day [6]. Even several vaccines in one day expose your child to a tiny amount of germs compared to what they encounter in their environment [6].

**What if my child missed a vaccine?**
Contact your GP to catch up [2]. It is important that vaccines are given on time for the best protection, but you can still catch up on most vaccines if you miss them [2][5].

**Why does my child need vaccines at specific ages?**
The vaccine schedule is designed to give your child protection at exactly the right time [1]. It allows their body to build up immunity before being exposed to very serious diseases [1].
