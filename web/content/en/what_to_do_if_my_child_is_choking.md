## What it is
Choking happens when food or a small object gets caught in your child's throat and blocks their airway [1]. This stops oxygen from reaching the lungs and brain [1]. If the brain goes without oxygen for more than four minutes, it can cause brain damage or death [1]. Young children are at especially high risk [1]. Common choking hazards include hot dogs, nuts, grapes, toy pieces, and coins [1]. Keep these items out of reach and always supervise your child while they eat [1].

## What you can do at home
Quick action can save a life when someone is choking [1]. Learn these skills before an emergency happens:
- Back blows [1]
- The Heimlich maneuver (abdominal thrusts) [1]
- CPR [1]

Do not attempt any first aid technique unless you have been properly trained. If your child is choking and you are not trained, call emergency services immediately. For infants and young children, techniques differ from adults, so use only age-appropriate methods you have learned from a certified course.

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Call emergency services right away if your child cannot breathe, cough, speak, or make sounds [1]. Also call if their lips or skin turn blue, they lose consciousness, or the object does not come out after first aid attempts [1]. Even if the object comes out, seek medical help if your child has a persistent cough, wheezing, or trouble swallowing afterward [1]. Do not wait to see if symptoms improve on their own.

## Common questions
**Why are young children more likely to choke?**
Their airways are smaller, and they often put objects in their mouths [1]. Foods like hot dogs, nuts, and grapes are common culprits, as are toy pieces and coins [1].

**Can I prevent choking?**
Yes. Keep small objects and hazardous foods out of reach, and supervise your child during meals [1]. Cut foods into small, safe pieces and avoid giving high-risk items to very young children [1].

**What if my child is choking but can still cough?**
Encourage them to keep coughing. Do not interfere if they can cough forcefully or breathe [1]. Only act if they cannot cough, speak, or breathe [1].

**Do I need formal training?**
Yes. Taking a certified first aid course that covers back blows, the Heimlich maneuver, and CPR is the best way to be prepared [1]. Practice on mannequins so you know what to do in a real emergency [1].
