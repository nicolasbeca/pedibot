## What it is

Flu is a viral illness that can be more dangerous for children than the common cold [2]. Symptoms come on very quickly, often within a few hours [3]. In children, flu can cause a sudden high temperature, an aching body, feeling tired or exhausted, a dry cough, a sore throat, a headache, difficulty sleeping, and loss of appetite [3]. Children may also have diarrhoea, tummy pain, feeling sick, or vomiting, although these are less common in adults [3]. Children can also get pain in their ear, sore eyes, swollen glands, and appear less active [3].

Children younger than 5 years old, especially those younger than 2, are at higher risk of serious flu complications [1][2]. Children of any age with chronic health conditions like asthma or diabetes are also at higher risk [1]. Complications can include sinus problems, ear infections, pneumonia, dehydration, and worsening of long-term medical problems [5]. In rare cases, flu complications can lead to death [5].

## What you can do at home

If your child has a high temperature and is uncomfortable, you can give them children's paracetamol or children's ibuprofen [10]. Always check the leaflet to make sure it is suitable for your child [10]. You can also try saline nose drops from a pharmacy if your child's nose is blocked [10].

Make sure your child drinks lots of fluids [10]. For babies, try smaller feeds more often [10]. For older children, give extra water or diluted fruit juice [10].

To help stop the spread of flu to others:
- Wash hands often with warm water and soap [4].
- Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue when you cough or sneeze [4].
- If you do not have a tissue, cough or sneeze into the bend of your elbow, not your hand [4].
- Bin used tissues as quickly as possible [4].

Try to stay at home and avoid contact with other people if you have a high temperature, feel hot, cold, or shivery, or do not feel well enough to do your normal activities [4]. You are more likely to give flu to others in the first 5 days after getting infected [4].

The flu vaccine helps protect against flu and is safe and effective [4]. Annual flu vaccination is recommended for everyone 6 months and older [1][6]. It is especially important for children at higher risk of complications [1]. For most people who need only one dose, September and October are generally good times to be vaccinated [7]. Some children need two doses, given at least four weeks apart [7]. It takes about two weeks after vaccination for the body to develop protection [7].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Flu can lead to serious complications in children [5]. Contact your healthcare provider promptly if your child has flu symptoms and is at higher risk for complications [8]. Antiviral treatment for flu works best when started very soon after symptoms begin, ideally within 2 days [8][9]. Treatment may reduce the risk of complications, including those that can result in hospitalization [8].

Seek medical care if your child shows signs of serious illness. These can include dehydration, which happens when the body loses too much water and salts [5]. Also watch for worsening of long-term medical problems like heart disease or asthma [5]. In rare cases, flu complications can lead to death [5].

## Common questions

**Can my child get the flu vaccine if they are under 6 months old?**
No. Flu vaccination is recommended for everyone 6 months and older [1][6]. Infants younger than 6 months are too young for the vaccine [1]. They rely on those around them to be vaccinated to help protect them from flu [1].

**Is the nasal spray flu vaccine an option for my child?**
The nasal spray vaccine is approved for people 2 through 49 years old [6]. However, it is not recommended for people with certain underlying medical conditions [6]. Flu shots are approved for people 6 months and older [6]. Talk to your healthcare provider about which vaccine is right for your child.

**Do antibiotics help treat the flu?**
No. Antibiotics don't work against viruses and won't help your child feel better [9]. Flu is caused by a virus [9]. Antiviral treatments are available for flu and work best when started very soon after symptoms begin [9].
