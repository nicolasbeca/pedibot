## What it is
Diarrhoea and vomiting are common in children and babies. They are often caused by a stomach bug or food poisoning [1][6]. Diarrhoea usually stops within 5 to 7 days. Vomiting usually stops in 1 or 2 days [5]. The advice is the same whether your child has diarrhoea and vomiting together or separately [1].

The main risk is dehydration. This means the body loses more fluids than it takes in [8]. Babies and children under 5 have a higher risk of dehydration [3][8]. Signs of dehydration in a child include: feeling thirsty, a dry mouth, dark yellow or strong-smelling pee, peeing less often than usual, feeling dizzy or tired, and sunken eyes [8]. In a baby, signs can include: a sunken soft spot on the top of the head, sunken eyes, few or no tears when crying, fewer wet nappies than usual, and being drowsy or irritable [8].

## What you can do at home
The most important thing is to give lots of fluids to avoid dehydration [1].

- Give small sips of water or squash often. Take small sips if your child feels sick [1].
- Carry on breastfeeding or bottle feeding your baby. If they are being sick, try small feeds more often than usual [1].
- For babies on formula or solid foods, give small sips of extra water between feeds. If your baby is under 6 months, boil the water first and let it cool [2].
- Ask your pharmacist about oral rehydration solution. Give regular small sips to replace lost fluids [2].
- Give small children their usual diet when they feel able to eat [2]. It may help to avoid fatty or spicy foods [1].
- Give paracetamol if your child is in discomfort. Check the leaflet before giving it [1].

Do not:
- Do not make formula weaker. Use it at its usual strength [2][5].
- Do not give fruit juice or fizzy drinks. They can make diarrhoea or vomiting worse [2][5].
- Do not give children under 12 medicine to stop diarrhoea [5].
- Do not give aspirin to children under 16 [5].

To stop the infection spreading, wash hands with soap and water often. Wash any clothing or bedding with poo or vomit on it separately on a hot wash. Clean toilet seats, flush handles, taps, surfaces and door handles every day. Do not share towels or cutlery. Keep your child off school until they have not been sick or had diarrhoea for at least 2 days [7].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department
Call 111 now if:
- You are worried about a baby under 12 months [4].
- Your child stops breast or bottle feeding while they are ill [4].
- A child under 5 has signs of dehydration, such as fewer wet nappies [4].
- Your child keeps being sick and cannot keep fluid down [4].
- Your child has bloody diarrhoea or bleeding from the bottom [4].
- Your child has diarrhoea for more than 7 days or vomiting for more than 2 days [4].

Call 999 or go to A&E if your child:
- Vomits blood or has vomit that looks like ground coffee [4].
- Has yellow-green or green vomit [4].
- May have swallowed something poisonous [4].
- Has a stiff neck and pain when looking at bright lights [4].
- Has a sudden, severe headache or sudden, severe tummy ache [4].
- Has blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin, lips or tongue. On brown or black skin, this may be easier to see on the palms or soles [4].
- Has severe difficulty breathing or is taking lots of quick, short breaths [4].
- Is confused or not responding as usual [4].

For infants and children, contact a provider right away if there is a change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy. Also seek help for diarrhoea lasting more than a day, any fever in infants, high fever in older children, frequent loose stools, vomiting often, or severe pain in the belly or rectum. Watch for signs of dehydration such as no wet diapers for 3 hours or more, or no tears when crying. Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus also need urgent attention [10].

## Common questions
**How long will it last?**
Diarrhoea usually stops within 5 to 7 days. Vomiting usually stops in 1 or 2 days [5].

**Can I give medicine to stop the diarrhoea?**
No. Do not give children under 12 medicine to stop diarrhoea [5]. A pharmacist can advise on other options for children over 5 [4].

**Should I stop feeding my baby?**
No. Carry on breastfeeding or bottle feeding. Try small feeds more often than usual [1]. Do not make formula weaker [2][5].
