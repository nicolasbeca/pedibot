## What it is

Poisoning happens when someone swallows, touches, or breathes in a substance that can harm the body [1][4]. Any substance can be poisonous if too much is taken [4]. Common causes include medicines like paracetamol or ibuprofen, household cleaning products like bleach or laundry detergent, alcohol and drugs, certain plants and mushrooms, pesticides, and carbon monoxide from gas appliances [6]. Effects can range from short-term illness to brain damage, coma, or death [4]. Symptoms may take a few hours or days to start, so get medical advice quickly if there is any chance of poisoning [6].

Symptoms can include feeling sick or being sick, diarrhoea, feeling sleepy, blurred vision, high temperature or feeling hot, cold, or shivery, confusion, difficulty breathing, seizures, or loss of consciousness [6].

## What you can do at home

If you think someone may have been poisoned, get medical advice immediately [1]. While waiting for help, you can do the following [2]:

- Start CPR if the person is unresponsive and not breathing.
- Put the person in the recovery position if they are unconscious but still breathing.
- Keep checking the person is breathing. If they are awake, keep talking to them to make sure they have not lost consciousness.
- Try to find what may have caused the poisoning. Look around for packaging, containers, or medicines.
- Take some of the substance or its packaging to show doctors at the hospital, if possible.
- If the person vomits, try putting some vomit in a container to give to healthcare professionals.

Do not do these things [2]:

- Do not try to make someone sick if they have swallowed something harmful. They could choke and stop breathing.
- Do not give the person anything to eat or drink.

To prevent poisoning at home, store medicines and chemicals out of reach of children, ideally in a locked cupboard [3]. Put safety catches on low cupboards that contain harmful substances like cleaning products [3]. Keep liquid cleaning products and chemicals in their original containers so they are not mistaken for drinks [3]. Read medicine instructions carefully to make sure you give the correct dose [3]. Get a carbon monoxide alarm for each room with appliances that burn gas, oil, coal, or wood [3]. Wear gloves, goggles, or a mask when handling harmful chemicals [3].

## When to see a doctor or go to the emergency department

Call 999 or go to A&E immediately if [1]:

- You think you, a child, or someone else may have swallowed, touched, or breathed in something harmful.
- Someone has lost consciousness.
- Someone has stopped breathing.
- Someone has severe difficulty breathing – they cannot get words out, or they are choking or gasping.
- Someone has a seizure (fit).

Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 for an ambulance [1]. Bring any medicines the person takes with you [1].

Call NHS 111 for urgent advice if you are not sure whether something swallowed, touched, or breathed in is harmful [1]. You can also call your local poison control center at 1-800-222-1222 right away if you suspect poisoning [4].

Hospital treatment may include antidotes, a charcoal drink, fluids given into a vein, or removing the substance from the body [7]. Even without symptoms, monitoring for several hours may be needed [7].

## Common questions

**Should I make my child vomit after swallowing something harmful?**
No. Do not try to make someone sick if they have swallowed something harmful. They could choke and stop breathing [2].

**Can food poisoning be treated at home?**
Food poisoning from food not prepared or stored properly is rarely serious and usually gets better on its own within a week [5]. However, this is different from other types of poisoning. If you are unsure whether something is harmful, get medical advice [1].

**What if symptoms have not started yet?**
Symptoms can take hours or days to appear. Get medical advice quickly if there is a chance of poisoning, even if the person feels fine [6].
